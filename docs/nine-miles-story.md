# Nine Miles to Nowhere — story canon

Fourth game. Premise shape: *Midnight Run* (a reluctant escort, a captive who is better at everything, everyone else wants him first) fused with *The Whole Nine Yards* (an ordinary professional, a kind retired hitman, and the people closest to the hero quietly rooting for him to fail). The Sierra flavour is Police Quest / Laura Bow: one town, one night, a case file. Game id `nine-miles`; SKUs `nine-miles` (full game) and `nine-miles-walkthrough`. Names and the setting are proposals for Sipes to confirm or change; nothing is implemented.

## Premise
Loomis Creek is a river town with a county fair, a courthouse, and one bail-bonds office. Dale Pruitt, 38, is the clerk at Marchetti Bail Bonds: the man who files the paperwork, not the man who brings anyone in. At 9:40 on the Friday night of the fair, his boss Gus calls from a hospital bed (gallstones, not gangsters) to say that their biggest bond, $200,000 on a protected witness named Vincent Carrow, forfeits at 9 a.m. unless Carrow is standing in Courtroom B, and that Carrow has just phoned the office to say he is at the Starlite Diner, he is hungry, and somebody should come and get him. Dale is the only somebody.

## The hook
Vincent Carrow, 61, retired from a career he describes as "logistics", is the State's witness against the Bellamy brothers, who run everything in the county that is not the fair. Vin does not need rescuing. He wants to testify. He also wants pie, he wanders off, he is physically incapable of being hurried, and he has hidden the ledger that convicts the Bellamys somewhere between the diner and the courthouse "for insurance". Dale's car dies in the diner lot. The courthouse is nine miles away. Between here and there: the Bellamys' crew, who want Vin dead before 9; Sheriff Royce Tubbs, who is paid to make sure it happens; the State's one honest investigator, who does not know Dale exists; and Marla Deacon, the county's only licensed bounty hunter, who would like to deliver Vin herself for the fee, and who is also Dale's ex-wife.

## Cast
- **Dale Pruitt** — hero. Bail-bonds clerk, divorced, careful, good with forms and bad with people, carrying a clipboard, a pair of handcuffs he has never used and a phone that Gus keeps calling.
- **Vincent "Vin" Carrow** — the witness. Retired, courtly, patient, an amateur birdwatcher with good binoculars. Can open any lock, read any room and charm any waitress, and will do so for Dale if asked nicely and if there is nothing more interesting nearby. Companion for the whole game.
- **Marla Deacon** — bounty hunter, Dale's ex-wife, competent and furious. Wants the fee and, on a bad night, wants Dale to see her get it. Becomes an ally in chapter 6 for her own reasons.
- **Gus Marchetti** — Dale's boss, on the phone from Loomis General, in pain, sentimental and threatening by turns. Never seen.
- **Sheriff Royce Tubbs** — on the Bellamy payroll. Runs the impound lot like a private bank. Polite to everyone he is about to ruin.
- **The Bellamy brothers' crew** — Lyle and Curtis Bellamy (never seen until chapter 8) and their men: Dutch, Mouse and Big Teddy, who are better at driving than at thinking.
- **Lorraine Keel** — owns the Keel & Anchor on the river. Vin's past. Has the one thing Vin cannot open: her good opinion.
- **Investigator Ada Okafor** — State Attorney's office, honest, exhausted, the person Vin is actually trying to reach.
- **Pip** — fifteen, runs the golf-cart shuttle at the fair, will trade anything for a working phone charger.
- **Earlene** — the Starlite's night waitress. Sees everything; says it for a tip.
- **Duchess** — Lorraine's elderly bloodhound. Finds people. Finds Vin, usually.

## Places
Loomis Creek on fair night: the Starlite Diner and its lot; the Riverside Motel; the county fairground; the Greyline bus depot; the Keel & Anchor bar on the river; the river crossing (ferry closed, railway bridge, a johnboat); the county impound lot; the courthouse steps and Courtroom B at nine o'clock.

## Summary
Dale's one night of fieldwork. He collects a witness who will not be collected, loses his car, and walks nine miles through a town where every light that is on belongs to someone looking for the man beside him. Vin opens doors and buys time and disappears twice; Dale fills in forms, lies badly, and learns to do the thing Vin keeps telling him, which is to stop apologising and decide. The ledger turns up in a bus-depot locker, then in Lorraine's icebox, then in Dale's own impounded car, which is where Vin put it in the diner lot while Dale was on the phone, because the one place nobody searches is a bail clerk's station wagon. Marla joins them on the river when the Bellamys make it clear the fee is not the only thing on offer. At 8:48 the three of them reach the courthouse steps with the ledger, the Bellamys, Tubbs and twelve minutes, and Dale has to get a witness through a metal detector that a sheriff controls. Vin testifies. Gus keeps the bond. Dale quits by text, which Gus does not read because he is being discharged. Vin gives him the binoculars.

## Chapters
1. **The Starlite Diner** (free scene) — Dale has to identify a hitman he has only seen on a bond form, get him to leave a slice of pie, tip Earlene enough to forget them both, and discover that his car is dead and the man in the corner booth is not a trucker.
2. **The Riverside Motel** — Vin's room is being watched by two of the Bellamys' men; Vin's binoculars, his "insurance" and his heart pills are inside, and Dale has to get them out without going through the door.
3. **The County Fair** — Nine miles on foot is too far; Pip's golf cart is the only transport, Vin has wandered off to the shooting gallery, and the sheriff's deputies at the gate have Dale's bond form in their hands.
4. **The Greyline Depot** — Locker 41 has the ledger, Marla has Dale's handcuffs, the last bus has Vin on it, and Dale has to choose between the fee and the witness while a bus pulls out.
5. **The Keel & Anchor** — Lorraine's bar is where Vin was always going; the ledger is in code, Lorraine has the key to it, and her price is a conversation Vin has been avoiding for twenty years, with Dale as the referee.
6. **The River** — The ferry is chained, the Bellamys are on both banks, Marla is on the railway bridge with a decision to make, and Dale rows a johnboat with a hitman, a bloodhound and his ex-wife in it.
7. **The Impound Lot** — Dale's wagon, the bond paperwork and the real ledger are inside Sheriff Tubbs's fenced lot; Vin breaks in, Dale forges his way out, and Tubbs makes a phone call that sets up the ending.
8. **Courtroom B** — Twelve minutes to nine. The steps, the metal detector, the Bellamys in the gallery, Tubbs at the door and Investigator Okafor on the far side of all of it. Dale, who has filled in forms his whole life, has to get the right one in front of the right person before the clock on the wall says the bond is gone.

## Tone
Night-time small-town noir with the lights on: neon, fairground music, a river, a lot of walking. Dale dies the way careful men do, by finally not being careful: the pie, the Ferris wheel, the river, the impound dog. Vin delivers most of the death lines, kindly. Marla delivers the rest. 250 points, 2–3 hours, ages 12+ (guns are present and never fired by the player; one punch, thrown by Marla).
