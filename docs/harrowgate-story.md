# The Harrowgate Will — story canon

Fifth game. Premise shape: *Clue* (a dinner party of blackmail victims, a host who dies at the table, a storm that locks everyone in, and more than one ending that fits the facts) in a 1928 country house, the Laura Bow corner of the portal. Game id `harrowgate`; SKUs `harrowgate` (full game) and `harrowgate-walkthrough`. Names are proposals for Sipes to confirm or change; nothing is implemented.

## Premise
Harrowgate House stands on a tidal island off the Norfolk coast, reached by a causeway that the sea covers twice a day. On the last Saturday of October 1928, Sir Lionel Harrowgate invites six people to dinner and the reading of a new will. Each of them is someone he has been quietly blackmailing for years. He also hires, through an agency, a stenographer to take down every word that is said: Edith "Edie" Marsh, 26, fast, invisible, and the only person at the table with nothing to hide and no reason to be there. The causeway floods at eight. The storm comes in at nine. Sir Lionel dies at the fish course.

## The hook
Sir Lionel's death is theatre. He has arranged with his doctor to be "poisoned" in front of his guests so that he can lie in the library, apparently dead, and listen to what six frightened people say about him and his will. The will vanishes from the library an hour later. At midnight, someone who has worked out that the corpse is breathing makes sure it stops. By dawn there is a real body, a missing will, a solicitor who has also stopped moving, six guests with motives, four servants with secrets, a storm that has cut the telephone, and a stenographer with a notebook that contains every word anyone has said since seven o'clock. The police boat comes with the tide at 6:40. Whoever did it intends to be on it.

## Cast
- **Edie Marsh** — hero. Agency stenographer, Pitman shorthand at 180 words a minute, a notebook, a pencil, a good memory for who said what and a bad habit of noticing. Nobody looks at the stenographer, which is her whole advantage.
- **Sir Lionel Harrowgate** — host, collector, blackmailer, dies twice. Keeps his files in the study behind a false Turner.
- **Dr Ambrose Pell** — Sir Lionel's physician, who administered the "poison" and signed nothing. Nervous, drinks, has a past in Bombay he is paying for.
- **Mrs Vivienne Lacombe** — widow, former actress, Sir Lionel's lover twenty years ago and the mother of a child he has never acknowledged. Glamorous, watchful, and the only guest who cries at the right moment.
- **Colonel Rupert Fane** — retired, bluff, owns the only gun in the house and says so. Lost a regiment's funds at cards; Sir Lionel has the IOUs.
- **The Reverend Thaddeus Mole** — rector of the mainland parish, soft, apologetic, embezzling the roof fund for reasons that turn out to be kind.
- **Mr Benedict Sallow** — the solicitor who drew the new will and the old one. Knows what is in both. Dies in chapter 4, or appears to.
- **Miss Honoria Quist** — Sir Lionel's secretary for six years, efficient, underpaid, and, Edie will discover, Vivienne Lacombe's daughter, cut out of both wills. The murderer, for reasons the game lets the player understand.
- **Garrick** — the butler. Thirty years' service, a ledger of everything that has ever been delivered to the house, and a loyalty that transferred, some time ago, from the master to the secretary.
- **Mrs Oakes** — the cook. Knows what was in the fish course. Knows what was not.
- **Tilly** — the housemaid, sixteen, terrified, Edie's one friend in the house and the only person who goes into every room.
- **Wilf** — the boatman, in the boathouse, drunk, and the only way off the island before the tide.
- **Gladstone** — Sir Lionel's tortoise, ninety years old, lives in the conservatory, and is sitting on the thing everyone is looking for.

## Places
Harrowgate House and its island: the causeway and the hall; the dining room; the library; the first-floor bedrooms and the gallery; the conservatory, cellars and the passage to the boathouse; the servants' hall and kitchens; Sir Lionel's study; the drawing room at dawn.

## Summary
Edie's longest night. She takes down a dinner that ends with the host face-down in the turbot, is told by the doctor it was his heart, and writes down that it was not. She sees the library door open at eleven and shut at midnight, and when she looks again the dead man is dead differently. She finds the will where a tortoise has been sitting on it, learns what is in it, and learns that the person it leaves out is the person who has been running the house. She follows the solicitor's second death to a wine cellar, a secret stair and a boatman who has been paid to be drunk. Tilly shows her the servants' side of every door. The study gives her the files, and the files give her six motives and one that is not a motive but a wound. At dawn the drawing room fills, the tide turns, the police boat is a speck, and Edie, who has never spoken at any of the meetings she has taken down, has to stand up and say who did it, and the game lets her get it wrong twice, plausibly, before she gets it right.

## Chapters
1. **The Causeway** (free scene) — Edie crosses to the island ahead of the tide, is received by a butler who did not expect her, finds her room, her instructions and a house where every guest is already lying about why they came, and takes down the first words of the evening before the sea closes the road.
2. **The Fish Course** — Dinner: seven at the table, Edie at a side table with her notebook, a toast, a will announced and not read, and Sir Lionel dead in the turbot; Edie takes down who moved, who did not, and what the doctor said that was not true.
3. **The Library** — The body laid out, the will in the desk, then not in the desk; Edie, forbidden the room, goes in anyway and finds the host warmer than a corpse should be, and the first of the guests with a reason to want the will gone.
4. **Upstairs** — The bedrooms and the gallery after midnight: a search of six rooms, a second scream, the solicitor dead on the stairs, and the library corpse now dead in earnest.
5. **The Conservatory** — Gladstone, the will, a passage to the cellars, the wine, the boathouse and Wilf; the will names an heir who is not in the house, and the passage explains how someone has been moving between floors without using the stairs.
6. **The Servants' Hall** — Garrick's ledger, Mrs Oakes's menu, Tilly's keys and the staff's side of the night; the solicitor is not dead, he is in the coal cellar with a lump on his head, and he tells Edie what the old will said.
7. **The Study** — Behind the false Turner: the files, six motives in six folders, a seventh folder with no name and a birth certificate in it, and Edie understanding who Honoria Quist is before Honoria finds her there.
8. **The Drawing Room** — 6:10 a.m., thirty minutes to the boat, everyone assembled because Edie asks Garrick to assemble them, and the accusation: three possible, two wrong and comic, one right and not comic at all, and the clock running to the moment the murderer walks down to the jetty.

## Tone
Laura Bow by candlelight with Clue's engine underneath: a storm, a map of the house, everyone lying. Deaths are period and frequent: the causeway, the gallery rail, the colonel's gun, the cellar stair, the tortoise (indirectly). Tilly and Garrick deliver most of the death lines. 250 points, 2–3 hours, ages 12+ (no blood on screen; two bodies, one of them temporary).
