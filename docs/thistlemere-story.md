# Crown of Thistlemere — story canon

Third game. Premise shape: *Game Night* (a staged game that turns real while everyone keeps applauding) poured into a King's Quest castle. Game id `thistlemere`; SKUs `thistlemere` (full game) and `thistlemere-walkthrough`. Names below are proposals for Sipes to confirm or change; nothing is implemented.

## Premise
Every Midwinter's Eve the court of Thistlemere plays the Usurper's Game: a one-night masquerade in which a pretend conspiracy "kidnaps" the King, and the courtiers race through the castle solving riddles to unmask the traitor before the dawn bell. The winner is crowned with a circlet of holly and sits at the King's right hand for a year. The game has been written for thirty years by the Lord Chamberlain. This year, as a public snub, he has handed the job of running it to the most junior person in the Heralds' Office.

## The hook
Ottoline Fenwick, Junior Herald and reluctant Master of the Game, watches four masked men carry King Berengar out of the Great Hall at the exact moment her script says they should. The court cheers. Her sister rolls her eyes. The problem is that Otti's four actors are in the buttery, asleep in a cask of the King's wine, and the men who took the King are not acting. Otti is the only person in Thistlemere who knows the game is real, and every time she tries to say so, somebody congratulates her on the realism.

## Cast
- **Ottoline "Otti" Fenwick** — hero. Junior Herald, 24, competent, overlooked, carries a satchel of sealed clue envelopes and a wax seal she is not technically allowed to use. Knows the castle's rules; learns its passages.
- **Lady Perpetua Fenwick** — Otti's older sister, the Queen's favourite lady-in-waiting, winner of the Usurper's Game six years running. Glamorous, quick, and not as unkind as Otti thinks. Does not believe a word of it until chapter 5.
- **King Berengar the Fond** — elderly, delighted by games, kidnapped for real. Spends the night tied up and enjoying himself. Owns a ferret called Chancellor.
- **Queen Alaric** — sharp, bored, sees more than she says. Has the second copy of the Great Seal.
- **Lord Chamberlain Severin Vosk** — wrote the Game for thirty years. Precise, patient, humiliated by a King who laughs at everything. The real conspirator: the Game is his cover for a forged abdication.
- **Duke Corvin of Ashvale** — loud, handsome, swaggering, the obvious villain. Is in fact playing the Game extremely hard and is as surprised as anyone. Red herring and, eventually, muscle.
- **Hobb** — Warden of the Postern Gate. Never invited to the Game in twenty years. Owns a key to every lock in the castle and a scrapbook of previous Games. Lonely, decent, a little frightening. Takes an arrow for Otti in chapter 4.
- **Dame Ursel** — court physician, asleep for most of the night, awake for the worst of it.
- **Sir Dunstan** — Captain of the Guard, who has been told the Game is on and therefore arrests nobody.
- **The Four Masks** — Vosk's hired men, in the costumes Otti sewed for her actors.
- **Chancellor** — the King's ferret. Knows every passage in the castle. Follows anyone holding a sausage.

## Places
Thistlemere Castle on Midwinter's Eve, under snow. The Heralds' Garret; the Great Hall; the Buttery and Kitchens; the Chapel and its crypt; the Queen's Tower; the Postern Gate and the frozen moat; the Chamberlain's Study; the Great Hall at dawn.

## Summary
Otti's worst Midwinter. The Game she was set up to fail at begins with a real abduction nobody will believe in. She finds her actors drunk, her clue envelopes rewritten in a hand that is not hers, and the King's ferret very upset. She follows the kidnappers' route through a castle full of courtiers in masks who keep handing her the clues she wrote. The Duke she suspects turns out to be an idiot; the Warden she fears turns out to be the only honest man in the building; her sister, finally, believes her, and the two Fenwicks do what they have never done before, which is work together. The trail ends in the Chamberlain's study with a forged abdication waiting for the Great Seal. At dawn the court gathers to crown the Game's winner, Vosk among them, and Otti has until the dawn bell to turn the Game's own last riddle against the man who wrote it. The King, untied and delighted, crowns her with the holly and declares it the best Game in thirty years, "though a little long."

## Chapters
1. **The Heralds' Garret** (free scene) — On the evening of the Game, Otti finds her master key gone, her clue envelopes resealed by someone else, and a window into the courtyard where four men in her costumes are unloading a cart that should be empty; she has to get her satchel, her seal and herself to the Great Hall before the first bell.
2. **The Great Hall** — The Game begins on cue, the King is carried off to applause, and Otti must prove to herself, if to nobody else, that the kidnapping was real by finding where her actual actors are.
3. **The Buttery and Kitchens** — Four drunk actors, an angry cook, a ferret in the sausages and the first real clue: the kidnappers' cart went down, not out, and the King's own ferret knows the way.
4. **The Chapel and the Crypt** — Following Chancellor into the passages under the chapel, Otti runs into the Duke of Ashvale running his own game, finds the kidnappers' camp, and learns the hard way that the Four Masks have real crossbows when Hobb, who has been following her, takes a bolt in the arm.
5. **The Queen's Tower** — Otti needs the Queen, and to reach the Queen she needs Perpetua; the sisters have their fight, then their truce, and Otti leaves with the Queen's copy of the Great Seal and a sister on her side.
6. **The Postern Gate and the Moat** — Hobb's gate is where the King will leave the castle; Otti, Perpetua and a reluctant Duke stage a rescue on the ice using the Game's own props, and get the King back, but not the forged abdication.
7. **The Chamberlain's Study** — In Vosk's rooms Otti finds thirty years of Games, the real plan, and the forgery, and understands that Vosk never needed the King gone: he needed the Game to end with Vosk crowned, so the court would witness the "abdication" and cheer.
8. **Dawn** — The court assembles for the crowning with the dawn bell twelve minutes off; Otti, Master of the Game, must run the final riddle live so that the court itself unmasks Vosk before he can produce the sealed paper.

## Tone
King's Quest castle, Game Night energy. Snow, candles, masks and a ferret. Deaths are frequent, reversible and courtly: the moat, the crypt stair, the Duke's temper, the kitchen's Christmas goose. Otti's sister narrates several of them with real affection. 250 points, 2–3 hours, ages 10+.
